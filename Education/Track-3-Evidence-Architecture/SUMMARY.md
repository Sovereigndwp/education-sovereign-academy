# Track 3 — Summary and verdict

2026-09-04 · Composable Evidence Architecture + AI-Era Evidence Pack (Algebra I: Linear Functions), sample v0.1

## Verdict

**YES** — we now have a free artifact worth cold-sending to secondary-math curriculum directors, subject to one pre-send step that is not structural: Dalia reads the twelve items as a teacher (README item 5), because the pack's footer says a working teacher checked it and that must be true before it goes out.

**Strongest reason for the verdict.** The pack passes the test that the free deliverable has been failing since Track 1 — it is visibly *not a generator output* — while giving away real, complete value. A director who forwards it hands her Algebra I teachers three keyed, copy-permitted check pages plus twenty-four extra forms and the rules to write more; and the same document shows her, without a pitch, exactly what a paid version adds that a cold artifact cannot: the map written against *her department's own* assignments, coverage counted across a unit and a course, and the uncovered claims listed. Nothing is withheld; the scaling is legible; the one claim no cheap check reaches is stated rather than papered over.

**What the recipient gets that they could use tomorrow.** Pages 4–6: three one-page Evidence Checks on linear functions (12 items: two-point equation, find b, spot the slope error, solve for days; candle rate/start swap, taxi perturbation, two-plan prediction, construct a line to constraints; table→equation, equation→table with intercepts, "which tables are linear" with an uneven x-step, hiker transfer), each usable as an exit ticket, warm-up or quiz component, all keys verified. Page 9: two more forms of every item so period 6 doesn't get period 1's ticket, and the vary/fix/avoid rule for writing a fourth. Page 3: a two-level scoring rule and three class-level patterns with a next move. About three hours of careful item-writing a teacher would not otherwise spend, ready at 7:45 a.m.

## What was built

- **Ontology v0.1** on established structure (evidence-centred design; the Sydney two-lane model; Corbin et al. 2025): Learning Claim → Existing Activity (roles) → Delegability (three values, reasons, never a number) → Evidence Gap → Verification Primitive → Verification Item → Evidence Statement (supports / cannot support) → Cost. Three *required* evidence conditions — independent, fresh, aimed — and the rest demoted to properties. Eight primitives (Perturb, Transfer, Predict, Diagnose, Represent, Reverse, Generate, Classify); "Explain" rejected as a standalone. The variant rule (vary/fix/key/avoid) is the field that makes "fresh" operational.
- **Schema + 8 records**, 12 items × 3 forms, all 36 keys recomputed by script; one claim (LC-8, judging a linear model against real data) recorded as having no cheap independent check.
- **The pack**: 10 pages, 7 of them usable material.
- **Three stress tests.** Ontology (04): four failures found and handled at field level; no v0.2. "Just a quiz" (07): the student pages alone *are* close to a quiz bank; the architecture lives on pp. 2, 3, 7–9, and five revisions moved it in front of the items. Commercial (08): both director tests pass; the strongest case against is "my teachers already do exit tickets," answered on p. 3 and p. 9 only if she reads them.

## The two honest weaknesses

1. The sample maps *typical* assignments, not the teacher's own — the structural limit of a cold artifact and precisely the paid product's opening.
2. Every cost is modelled; every delegability judgment is unrun; demand is untested. The plan's behavioural gates still decide everything.

## The blindspot to flag

The pack is good enough to be forwarded *and* good enough to be copied. Its most distinctive page (fresh-form rules) is also the easiest to reproduce in form by a generator; what cannot be copied — verified keys across forms, distractors that carry a named error, and the restraint to leave LC-8 empty — is invisible until a competitor's key is wrong in front of a class. The moat is real and it is quiet. Do not expect the pack to *explain* the moat; expect the department engagement to demonstrate it.
