# Practice, Production, Evidence

A complimentary classroom-ready sample from The Sovereign Academy

### The distinction in plain language

Most assignments do three jobs at once. They give students **practice** (the doing that builds the skill). They ask for a **production** (the page, the graph, the write-up). And the teacher reads that production as **evidence** of what the student can do.

The first two jobs still work. The third one has quietly changed. When outside help — a tutor, a parent, or now an AI that answers from a photo — can produce the artifact, the artifact stops telling you who did the thinking. The homework is still good practice. It is just weaker evidence.

This pack does not try to make assignments "AI-proof." It separates the jobs. Keep your practice and your productions. Then add a few short, supervised, fresh checks — each aimed at one specific claim about what a student can do — and read *those* as your independent evidence.

Each check says what it can show and what it cannot. None of them proves mastery on its own. Together, across a unit, they let you say what you know about each student and what you still don't.

**One example.** A student hands in a modelling worksheet: taxi fare <span class="mono">F = 2m + 3</span>, with the sentence "3 is the pickup fee and 2 is the cost per mile." Good practice. But the sentence is the most reproducible text in the course — a photo of the worksheet returns it in one turn. Even a "show your thinking first" step doesn't settle it: a prediction sheet, once written, can itself be photographed and used to generate work that agrees with it.  
  
Now, in class, with no warning: *"The pickup fee has doubled and the per-mile rate hasn't changed. Which part of the equation changes? Write the new equation."* Two minutes. Ten seconds to score. A student who wrote <span class="mono">F = 2m + 6</span> attached the fee to the right parameter, on their own, today. That is a small claim — and it is theirs.

## Running a check tomorrow

### Three conditions that make a check independent evidence

- **Independent.** Done in the room, under your eye, no devices. This is the whole point; a check taken home is just more practice.
- **Fresh.** The exact form has not been available to any student beforehand. Page 9 gives two extra forms per check and a rule for writing more, so period 6 does not get period 1's ticket.
- **Aimed.** Each check targets one named claim. A response that can't be mapped to a claim is a score, not evidence.

Everything else — length, format, whether a calculator is allowed — is a cost or a design choice. It changes what the check is evidence *of*, not whether it is evidence.

### Scoring: two levels, then move on

Every key on pp. 7–8 gives a **counts as evidence** / **does not yet** rule, plus one **flag** pattern worth a 20-second conversation. There is no partial credit because you are not grading; you are deciding whether you have evidence for a claim. Most items score in 5–20 seconds per student. A full page for a class of 30 is about 10 minutes.

When a selection is paired with a one-line reason, **the reason decides**. A circled answer alone has a guess rate and is never counted.

### Ways to use the pages

- **Exit ticket:** one item, last 3 minutes, collect on the way out.
- **Warm-up:** one item on the board, mini-whiteboards, read the room in 60 seconds (EC-07 and EC-08 work especially well this way).
- **Check page:** a whole student page (4 items, 8–10 minutes) as a quiz component or a mid-unit read.
- **Rows or periods:** hand out forms A, B, C by row. Same claim, different numbers, same key structure.

### Anatomy of a check

Every check is deliberately narrow. Twelve narrow claims you can stand behind are more useful than one broad grade you can't; aggregate across the unit, not within an item. This pack is not a detector (it never looks at student work) and nothing in it is "AI-resistant" — nothing is.

## Fresh-form rules — so no student can prepare the exact page

Every check is a template. The rule says what may change and what must not; Forms B and C are ready to use. Keys were recomputed by script before printing. Use different forms by row, or by period, or write your own from the rule.

Why this page matters: "fresh" is one of the three conditions on p. 3. A check that any student could have seen in advance is practice, not evidence. The rules make freshness something you can produce in two minutes rather than something you hope for.

## What a full Evidence Architecture would add

This sample is one topic, mapped by hand: seven claims, twelve checks, one evidence gap left honestly open. Every check here was written from the same record — claim, existing activity, gap, primitive, item, evidence statement, fresh-form rule, cost — so the same structure scales without changing shape.

**one topic**  →  **unit**  →  **course**  →  **department**

| Level                   | What the map contains                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | What it lets a department say                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Topic** (this sample) | Claims for one topic; your existing assignments tagged by role and delegability; the evidence gap per claim; a check per gap with its scope stated; fresh-form rules.                                                                          | "For linear functions, here is what our homework still shows, here is what it no longer shows on its own, and here is the 10 minutes a week that covers the difference."                                            |
| **Unit**                | The same for every topic in the unit, using the department's own assignments rather than typical ones; a unit evidence map showing which claims have independent evidence and which rest only on take-home work or the unit test.              | "Before the test, we have independent evidence on 14 of 19 claims; the five that don't have it are these, and here is why (two of them have no cheap check)."                                                       |
| **Course**              | Every unit; prerequisite links between claims so a "not yet" in October points to the claim that failed in September; the total added class time and scoring time for the year, stated per teacher.                                            | "Our Algebra I evidence plan costs each teacher about X minutes of class time and Y minutes of scoring per week. Here is the number, and here is what we removed to pay for it."                                    |
| **Department**          | Course maps for Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II; a shared library of checks with fresh-form rules; a department statement of which claims are evidenced independently and how — the outcome evidence that PD funding increasingly has to show. | "Here is the share of our learning claims that rest on independent evidence, by course, and how it changed this year." A number a department can defend line by line, because every line has its reason next to it. |

### What stays the same at every level

- Homework stays. Practice is not the problem. The architecture adds targeted evidence where the inference is weak; it never asks a department to make assignments "AI-resistant."
- Every check states what it cannot show. The map never implies mastery from a single observation.
- Every cost is on the page. A check that a department admires and cannot afford in minutes is not a check.
- Some claims will have no cheap check. The map says so, and says where the evidence has to come from instead.
- No student work, no student data, no software, no detector. The unit of work is the assignment and the claim.

### What this sample is, and is not

It is a working sample: an Algebra I teacher can copy pp. 4–6 and run one check tomorrow. The claims are aligned to CCSS Algebra I standards; the checks rest on established assessment design (evidence-centred design; the Sydney "two-lane" model of secured and open assessment; erroneous examples; learner-generated examples; multiple representations). It is not a research instrument, not a mastery measure, not a detector, and not "AI-resistant" — nothing is. A student who "counts" on all twelve has given you twelve narrow, independent observations, not a claim of mastery of the unit; LC-8 has no item here, on purpose. Time estimates are modelled; a teacher who runs a check will have a better number than ours, and we would like to hear it.
