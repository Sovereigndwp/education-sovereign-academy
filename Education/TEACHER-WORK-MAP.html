The modeled teacher, and how to read the numbers

A full-time secondary math teacher: 5 sections, ~140 students, a ~54-hour actual work week of which roughly 46% is spent teaching S. The rest — planning, grading, differentiation, communication, documentation — is the surface we're mapping. Hour figures are per teacher per week. Sourced ranges carry S; where no clean study exists I model a conservative point estimate and tag it A. The point is not decimal precision; it's the shape of the week and which slabs are both large and safely reducible.

Reliability caveat, stated up front. Self-reported teacher time is noisy and studies disagree (grading alone is reported anywhere from ~5 to ~10 hrs/week). Every recommendation below is therefore built to be re-measured with a real baseline time-diary in the pilot, not taken from these figures. A number you can defend beats a number that impresses.

Recommended pilot — the smallest three that prove the thesis

A · Differentiate-this  +  B · Assessment & key builder  +  C′ · Targeted reteach from teacher-reported misconception.

These three share the one property the pilot's thesis demands: they operate on the teacher's own materials and a teacher-supplied signal — never on raw student records. That makes "we didn't give AI uncontrolled authority" trivially true and provable: no student PII ingested in v1, every output reviewed, nothing student-facing auto-ships. They are also the three highest composites, the three most cleanly measurable, and the three best matched to assets you already own.

Why each earns its place

  • A · Differentiate-this — your strongest asset advantage. The "same objective, same cognitive demand, different access" guarantee is build-curriculum's conceptual-model gate applied to differentiation, and no generator has it. High pain (IEP/ELL every day), highest WTP (equity budgets), low data risk. This is the headline.
  • B · Assessment + key builder — the credibility floor. Easiest to build, essentially zero privacy risk (no student data), the cleanest before/after minutes of anything on the map. It pairs with A (same lesson → aligned quiz + key) so the pilot feels like one coherent package, not three gadgets.
  • C′ · Targeted reteach from teacher-reported misconception — the moat, minus the risk. The teacher tells it, or picks from a menu, "9 students confused denominator size with fraction size," and the agent produces the 8-minute opener + practice that targets it. This puts your misconception-intelligence engine — your real differentiator — into the pilot, while the evidence is teacher-supplied, so there is no student-record ingestion and the teacher stays the authority on what was misunderstood.

Conservative recoverable total: ~3–4 teacher-hours/week across the three, mostly from differentiation and reteach/assessment prep — enough to clear a "100 hours across a department in a term" floor, without touching grading authority or student data in v1.

What this decides, per your instruction. The fractions diagnostic is deliberately NOT one of the first three — the misconception engine enters as a teacher-facing reteach generator (C′), not a student-facing diagnostic. And the School AI Constitution is not designed first: this map tells us it only needs to govern four things in v1 — the cognitive-demand invariant (A), assessment integrity (B), the "teacher-reported signal, no PII" data boundary (C′), and "nothing student-facing auto-ships." Scope the Constitution to exactly that, once the pilot confirms it.

The strongest argument against my recommendation

The steelman: I picked the safe basket and benched the moat. The one workflow that hurts most, recovers the most hours, and is most uniquely yours is C — grading real student open-response and clustering the actual misconceptions. By excluding raw student work from v1, I:

  • Dodge the biggest pain and the biggest hours. Grading is what makes teachers quit (⅓ considered leaving over it). A pilot that leaves it untouched may recover ~3 hours of prep-side time that teachers experience as "nice," not "life-changing."
  • Bench the actual differentiator. Differentiation and assessment-generation (A, B) are exactly what MagicSchool and Diffit already do. Running the misconception engine on teacher-reported signals (C′) is a pale version of running it on real student responses (C) — the latter is the thing no competitor can match and the reason a school would pay you rather than them. I may be leading with commodity and hiding the crown jewel in v2.
  • Lead with the most fragile guarantee. "Preserve cognitive demand under differentiation" (A) is the hardest promise to keep and fails invisibly, while the most defensible capability (misconception diagnosis on real work) sits benched. I've front-loaded technical fragility and back-loaded technical strength.
  • Risk an underwhelming number. If the honest recoverable hours from A+B+C′ come in at 2–3/week and much of it is "work they weren't doing before," the guarantee is thin — whereas grading time is unambiguously real and large.

A serious person could conclude: make C the centerpiece, solve the data-governance properly up front (that's your Ariadna strength anyway), and lead the pilot with the one capability that is both the deepest pain and the deepest moat.

My measured response — why I still lead with A + B + C′

The steelman is right about the prize and wrong about the sequence. Three reasons I still defer C to v2:

  • The pilot's job is to prove the thesis, not to show the fanciest trick. The promise is "measurable time back without uncontrolled authority." Nothing tests "authority" harder than letting AI evaluate real student work — and nothing risks a trust-ending privacy incident faster. Winning the safe pilot earns the data-sharing agreement that makes C safe. Losing a student-data incident in month one ends the relationship before any hours are booked.
  • C′ already carries the moat into the room. The misconception engine is in the pilot; it just runs on a teacher-supplied signal instead of raw PII. Teachers see the differentiator; you don't take the risk. C then becomes the obvious, pre-sold v2 upgrade once the Constitution and data agreements exist — which this very map says to scope from what v1 reveals.
  • Governance is your unlock, but it isn't free or instant. Doing C right needs data-processing agreements, no-training guarantees, residency, and audit trails. Those take weeks and legal review. Gating the whole pilot on them means no pilot this term. A+B+C′ can start now and generate the number that funds the harder build.

So: lead safe, prove the number, earn the trust, then make C the headline of v2 — with the honest caveat that if a friendly school will sign a data agreement up front, promoting C into the first three becomes the stronger play, and I'd change the recommendation.
