Sovereign Classrooms — Who's Buying, and the Gap We Can Own

Agentic market scan of the Pre-K–12 teacher population: who is searching for and supplying content, what they already pay for, and the one gap the whole market leaves open — the gap the tyb-engine was built for.

Demand ledger + competitive map + willingness-to-pay · 2026-08-27 · input to the cross-subject transformation demo

The finding in one line. Every major tool generates content (MagicSchool, Diffit, Eduaide) or drills it (Boom, Kahoot, IXL), and two of them now transform your material into interactive activities (Curipod, NOLEJ). None of them diagnose why a specific student is wrong — and 2025–26 research shows generic LLMs actively misdiagnose struggling-student errors. That diagnosis — misconception-first, with validated detection signals and telemetry — is exactly what the tyb-engine already does. The wedge is not "make it interactive." It is "make it interactive and diagnostic."

Two teacher populations, both live: suppliers (2.6M on TPT, a $253M payout economy) and the AI-shy majority (49-hr weeks, 44% burned out, already spending ~$1k/yr of their own money). The demand for "AI without having to learn it" is proven — 850+ districts bought $5M+ of AI tools in the last six months — but it is fragmented and, critically, thin on diagnosis.

The research backs the wedge. 2025–26 work (the DrawEduMath study; the EDGE misconception-aware framework) finds that general vision-language / LLM tools underperform with struggling students and misdiagnose their errors. So the crowded part of the market (generation) is exactly where the incumbents are weakest at the thing that matters — knowing what the student actually got wrong. The tyb-engine's hand-built, validated misconception classifiers (e.g. unit-circle: axis-swap vs. impulsive vs. near-miss, each with a detection signal) are the direct answer.

The wedge, stated as a product

"Upload your own lesson. Get back an interactive that teaches it, tells you which misconception each student hit, and runs itself in class — and you never had to learn AI." The teacher stays sovereign: the AI proposes and delivers; they rule. Everyone else stops at "here's an activity"; we continue to "here's why Diego missed, and here's the re-teach."

Other gaps I spotted (you asked me to flag these)

1 · The misconception dashboard for schools

The single most defensible, district-sellable asset: aggregate the telemetry into a live misconception heatmap by class/grade/standard. Incumbents can't build it because they never diagnose at the item level. This is a data product, not a content product — higher margin, stickier, harder to copy.

2 · "Assessment of real learning" in the AI era

Teachers' #1 AI fear is that they can no longer tell what a student actually knows. A misconception-diagnosing interactive is an authentic-assessment tool. Reframing the product this way turns the biggest objection to AI into our headline benefit.

3 · Special-ed / IEP evidence engine

SPED teachers are the most underserved and the best funded. Telemetry auto-generates IEP progress evidence and differentiation on demand. A vertical product on its own.

4 · The AI-shy onboarding wedge

The scared-of-AI segment is the largest and least served. "AI without learning it" as a guided, zero-prompt onboarding (you upload, it does the rest) is a distinct acquisition wedge — and a natural fit for a teacher-PD / whole-school rollout offer.

Recommendation for the demo

Build the cross-subject transformation demo on Grade 3–5 fractions: largest buyer volume, richest and best-documented misconception set, visually interactive, your authentic subject, and a direct parallel to the unit-circle asset so the diagnosis story is undeniable. Show the full loop — upload a plain fractions worksheet → interactive placement/partition game → live misconception detection (equal-parts error, larger-denominator error, number-line misplacement) → teacher dashboard. Hold special-ed differentiation as the on-stage "and here's how the same engine serves your hardest-to-reach students," because that's where the district money and the moral weight both are.
