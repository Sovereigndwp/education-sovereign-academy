## Pushback — strongest arguments against my own design choices

**1. The cost line may be the thing that stops adoption, not the thing that enables it.**
I put "this costs you +35 class minutes and 0 review minutes" on every audit because §11 says the teacher-minutes risk is the biggest known risk and the plan says to test it directly. The strongest argument against: a teacher reading "+35 minutes" on page two may stop there, when a teacher who *ran* it would have found it fine. We are making the cost salient before the value is felt. The counter-counter: hiding the cost and discovering non-adoption at week six is exactly the failure the plan predicts, and a colleague who tells you the price is more trusted than one who doesn't. I still think the line belongs — but it belongs *after* the redesign, phrased as an estimate to be corrected, which is where I put it. If Phase 0 teachers react to the number and not the redesign, move it into Section 6 prose and soften nothing else.

**2. Both worked examples "pay for" the mechanism by cutting something — and that subtraction is where teachers will say no.**
The math example drops a generic paragraph (cheap; probably fine). The history example cuts essay length by ~25% and drops MLA. A history teacher whose district expects 800 words, or who uses this essay to teach MLA, will reject the subtraction and then the honest net is +1 min/essay, +2.5 h/cycle at 140 students — the §11 failure. The alternative is to *not* subtract and show the true added cost; that is more honest about the mechanism and less attractive as a redesign. I chose subtraction because the principle "a redesign is only adoptable if it ships with what makes the verification cheap" is the plan's own through-line, and in a service phase without the work-package agent, subtraction is the only cheap-making tool we have. Record whether contrast-cell teachers accept the cut; if most don't, the ELA/history cell has told you something important about the wedge, and about what the software eventually has to do.

**3. The three-band judgment is unfalsifiable as written, and I've only partly fixed that.**
"A current model can produce the whole artifact" is a claim about what a tool does; the plan's locked decision makes it a *professional judgment* rather than a measurement, which protects us from inventing a score but also means nobody can show we're wrong. I added the "Checked by" line so that the band can rest on an actual run of the assignment through two models rather than on Dalia's (or my) intuition. That costs ~10 minutes per audit on a 45–60 minute budget — roughly 20% of capacity across 100 audits, or 15–17 hours. The argument against doing it: the answer will be "whole artifact" nearly every time for take-home tasks, so we'd be spending 15 hours confirming the obvious. The argument for: the §8 objective-accuracy threshold measures whether we read the *teacher's intent* right, but nothing measures whether we read the *tool's capability* right — and that's the claim the redesign rests on. My recommendation: run it on every Phase-0 audit; if all five come back "whole artifact" at full marks, drop to spot-checks in Phase 1 and say "reviewer judgment, calibrated on N checks" in the artifact.

**Smaller pushbacks, briefly**
- *The optional worry field vs. the "unprompted" metric* (04 §C): the plan pre-registered a threshold that the form, as most people would build it, quietly breaks. Pick before Phase 1.
- *"First 100 by hand" is a promise Dalia makes to strangers.* At 45–60 minutes each, 100 audits is 75–100 hours on top of a teaching load. The outreach copy says "capped" so that stopping at 40 is honest; but a teacher told "first 100" who is number 41 and gets "we've stopped" will feel the bait. Consider "first hundred or so, until I've learned what I need" — vaguer and truer.
- *Community posts are a real channel risk.* Both major subreddits moderate research recruitment and solicitation; a removal is visible and a shadow-ban is not. Mod pre-clearance is cheap. I have not verified current rules — modeled.
- *The sprint metric is one we define, assign, and then report improving.* The blind re-judge is the only thing that makes it defensible to a board. If Dalia isn't willing to do that step in the first sprint, drop the metric from the page and sell the redesigns on their own merits.
- *The ELA/history example may be the better product.* The essay audit was easier to write with a stronger invariant, a clearer mechanism, and a lower net cost than the math one — and the pain evidence clusters there. The math wedge is justified by Dalia's credibility and the cleaner objective structure, not by pain. The contrast cell exists to test exactly this; don't let the 20-teacher cap keep it from being heard.
