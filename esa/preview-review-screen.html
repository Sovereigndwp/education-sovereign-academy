Fractions with Unlike Denominators

1 of 3 claims are carried as they stand. Here is each one, and what to do about the rest.

G5C1 · Does not ask for one part of it

Chooses a valid strategy for comparing two unlike fractions — equivalent fractions, a benchmark such as one half, or a visual model — executes it, and shows the reasoning that supports the symbol.

The assessment does not actually ask students to demonstrate one part of this.

Independent evidence that the student can arrive at the correct comparison for unlike fractions on novel number pairs under supervision.

  • Asked forSelects a way of comparing two fractions that do not share a denominator.item 1, 2, 3
  • Asked forCarries the chosen comparison through to a decision about which fraction is larger.item 1, 2, 3
  • Not askedWrites out the reasoning that justifies the comparison symbol the student selected.

What these results do not reach

  • That the student can show the reasoning that supports the symbol, since Part A instructs "Do not show work."
  • That any particular valid strategy — equivalent fractions, a benchmark such as one half, or a visual model — was the one the student used.
  • That a correct circle on any single item reflects reasoning rather than a correct answer reached another way, including selection among three symbols.

One item, changed. Same page, same item count, same minutes.

Item Part A, item 3 nowPart A - Compare. Circle the correct symbol. Do not show work. 3. 5/6 ( < ) ( > ) ( = ) 8/9
InsteadPart A - Compare. Circle the correct symbol. Items 1 and 2: do not show work. Item 3: circle the symbol, then finish the sentence on the line under it. 3. 5/6 ( < ) ( > ) ( = ) 8/9 I know because ________________________________________________ (one line: name the two numbers you compared after renaming, OR the benchmark you compared each fraction to, OR what your picture showed - then say which fraction that made larger).

The student must leave a written trace of the strategy that produced the symbol - the renamed pair (45/54 and 48/54), the distance of each fraction from 1 or from one half, or what a drawn model showed - so the circled symbol is no longer the only thing on the page; item 3 keeps the same number pair and the same comparison it already tested, and gives up only the roughly one minute of pure answer-speed that the 'do not show work' instruction was buying on this one item, while items 1 and 2 continue to supply the no-work comparisons unchanged.

G5C2 · Asks the right thing · the conditions limit what it proves

Constructs equivalent fractions with a common denominator, combines or compares like-sized parts, and simplifies or regroups to a mixed number where the item requires it.

The assessment asks for the right thing. Under these conditions the results cannot establish that each student can do it independently.

That the assessment asks for the whole of G5C2 across nine items and returns fully worked procedure produced under home conditions with resources available.

The declared supervision is "unsupervised" with an AI assistant, the internet and anything at home permitted, and the procedure this claim names is exactly what those permitted tools carry out, so the step-by-step work returned cannot be attributed to the student.

  • Asked forRewrites the given fractions as equivalent fractions sharing one denominatoritem 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8
  • Asked forAdds, subtracts, or orders the parts once they are the same sizeitem 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8
  • Asked forPuts the result in simplest form, or regroups an improper result into a mixed number when the item calls for oneitem 4, 5, 6, 7

What these results do not reach

  • That an individual student can construct common denominators and combine like-sized parts without tools or assistance.
  • That the shown steps record the student's own procedure rather than a reproduced one.
  • A summative individual judgment about procedural fluency with unlike denominators.

The assessment does not change. These returned pages support that the assignment asks for the whole of G5C2 across nine items and that a student hands back worked common denominators, combined parts, and simplest-form or mixed-number results, produced at home over a week with calculators, the internet and an AI assistant available. They do not support a summative individual judgment that a named student carried out that procedure herself.

If you need to be able to say each student can do it: one short check in the room, alongside this assignment, which stays exactly as it is.

Fill in the missing fraction. 2/3 + ______ = 1 1/12 Show your steps.

Counts as reaching the claim when the shown steps put both quantities over one common denominator, combine them, and the answer is given in simplest form. A correct answer with no common denominator visible in the steps, or steps that stop at an improper form the item's arithmetic required be regrouped, reaches only part of the claim.

To make a fresh version each period: MAY CHANGE: the given fraction and the target total (e.g. 3/4 + ___ = 1 1/8; 1/2 + ___ = 1 1/6). MUST STAY FIXED: two unlike denominators where neither is a multiple of the other where possible, a target written as a mixed number so the improper/mixed regrouping is unavoidable, and a missing fraction that exists in simplest form. KEY CHANGES: rewrite the target as an improper fraction over the common denominator and subtract the given fraction (13/12 - 8/12 = 5/12). AVOID: whole-number targets, targets where the missing fraction equals the given one, and denominator pairs already used on the take-home (2 and 3, 6 and 4, 3 and 4).

3 min for students · about 20 sec each to read · in the room, In class, proctored, individually, no notes, no devices, no calculator; fresh numbers each period from the variant rule.

G5C3 · Good evidence

States why adding denominators produces a wrong-sized part, and gives the correct sum.

This gives you evidence for what you want to know here. I would not change it.

Independent evidence that the individual student can state what is wrong with adding denominators and produce the correct sum, in the student's own written words.

  • Asked forSays in words what goes wrong when the denominators are added together.item 9
  • Asked forProduces the right answer to the addition that was done wrongly.item 9

What these results do not reach

  • That the student would catch the same error unprompted, since item 9 names the error's location by presenting the wrong answer.
  • That the student can diagnose fraction errors other than denominator-adding, which is the only error type this assessment presents.
  • Anything about the quality of mathematical language beyond what a 25-minute written response in Part D captures.

No change. Independent evidence that the individual student can state what is wrong with adding denominators and produce the correct sum, in the student's own written words.

The error presented in item 9 is the exact error the claim names, so the item is precisely targeted rather than broader than needed.
