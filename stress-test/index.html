A score can look precise while measuring the wrong thing.

A student can reach the right answer by recognising a familiar pattern, by following a rehearsed procedure, or by reasoning from a wrong model that happens to land on the correct number. The score is the same in every case. The evidence is not.

The Stress Test asks one question of every item: if a student gets this right, what do you actually know? We look for:

  • Questions that can be answered correctly without the intended understanding
  • Wrong models that produce the right answer — the false positive a score can’t see
  • Wrong-answer choices that reveal little about what to reteach
  • Items measuring something other than what you meant to measure
  • Places where a small change would make student reasoning visible

And when an item is sound, we say so. A stress test that always finds a crack is worthless.

How it works

  1. Submit one assessment you already use. Paste the text or attach a PDF. Tell us what students are supposed to understand. About three minutes.
  2. A teacher reads it. The analysis is machine-assisted and reviewed by Dalia Platt, a former high-school mathematics teacher, before anything reaches you. Expect it within one school day.
  3. You get one concrete finding, free. The item, what it’s trying to measure, what it may actually give evidence of, why that matters, and a stronger version you can use tomorrow — with a statement of what did not change.
  4. If you want the rest, the complete Stress Test of that assessment is $49. One assessment, one price. No subscription.

What we never do

  • No student work, no student names. We analyse the assessment, never student responses. The upload form says so, and we delete anything that arrives with student information.
  • The result belongs to you. We do not share it with your school, department, or anyone else without your written permission.
  • No training on your material. Your assessment is used to produce your finding and nothing else. You can ask for it to be deleted at any time.
  • No scores. Each finding is a named professional judgment with its reason attached, not a number we can’t defend.

The goal isn’t a harder test. It’s better evidence of what students actually understand.
