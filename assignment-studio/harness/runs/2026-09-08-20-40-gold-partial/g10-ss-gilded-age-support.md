## NEW VERSION

# Two Views of Wealth in the Gilded Age — Document Analysis

**Grade 10 U.S. History · Industrialization Unit · 45 minutes**

Read both documents. Answer the questions in Part A, then write the response in Part B.

---

**Document 1 — Andrew Carnegie, "Wealth," *North American Review*, June 1889 (excerpt)**

> The problem of our age is the proper administration of wealth, so that the ties of brotherhood may still bind together the rich and poor in harmonious relationship. The conditions of human life have not only been changed, but revolutionized, within the past few hundred years. … The contrast between the palace of the millionaire and the cottage of the laborer with us to-day measures the change which has come with civilization. This change, however, is not to be deplored, but welcomed as highly beneficial. … Much better this great irregularity than universal squalor.

**Document 2 — Preamble to the Omaha Platform of the People's (Populist) Party, July 1892 (excerpt)**

> We meet in the midst of a nation brought to the verge of moral, political, and material ruin. Corruption dominates the ballot-box, the Legislatures, the Congress, and touches even the ermine of the bench. … The fruits of the toil of millions are boldly stolen to build up colossal fortunes for a few, unprecedented in the history of mankind; and the possessors of these, in turn, despise the Republic and endanger liberty. From the same prolific womb of governmental injustice we breed the two great classes — tramps and millionaires.

---

### Part A — Sourcing and close reading

**Directions for Part A:** For questions 1–4, write short answers. Use the documents and what you know about the time period.

**1. Source each document**

For each document, identify:
- the **author** (who wrote it)
- the **year** (when it was written)
- the **audience** (who the author was most likely trying to reach)

Write one line for Document 1, one line for Document 2.

---

**2. Compare the two explanations**

Both authors describe the same fact: a large gap between the very rich and everyone else.

- In one sentence, state how **Document 1** explains where that gap came from.
- In one sentence, state how **Document 2** explains where that gap came from.

---

**3. Analyze word choice**

Choose one word or phrase from **Document 1** that shows Carnegie's attitude toward great fortunes. Explain what the word choice reveals.

Choose one word or phrase from **Document 2** that shows the Populist Party's attitude toward great fortunes. Explain what the word choice reveals.

---

**4. Explain why each author wrote as they did**

- Why might Carnegie, of all people, have written Document 1? (Think about his position in 1889.)
- Why might a farmers' and workers' party have written Document 2? (Think about their position in 1892.)

Use what you know about each author's situation and interests.

---

### Part B — Argument

**Directions for Part B:** Write a two-paragraph response to the question below. Use evidence from both documents and your knowledge of the period.

**5. Question:** In the late nineteenth century, did Americans see the growing gap between rich and poor as the natural price of progress, or as evidence that the system had failed?

**Your response should:**

- **Take a position** that accounts for **both** documents.
  - (You may argue that different groups saw it differently — if so, say which groups and why.)

- **Use evidence:** Include at least one piece of evidence from **each** document.

- **Weigh the sources:** Explain how the **point of view** of each author affects how much weight you give their claim.

- **Connect to the period:** Connect the documents to at least one specific development from the late 1800s.
  - Examples: the growth of trusts, the Homestead or Pullman strikes, farm debt, the 1893 depression.

**Length:** Two paragraphs.

---

**Scoring (6 points):**
- Sourcing accurate (1)
- Position addresses both documents (1)
- Evidence from each document (1)
- Point of view used to weigh evidence (1)
- Outside development connected accurately (1)
- Organization and clarity (1)
