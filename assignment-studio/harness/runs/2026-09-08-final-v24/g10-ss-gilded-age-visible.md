## NEW VERSION

# Two Views of Wealth in the Gilded Age — Document Analysis

**Grade 10 U.S. History · Industrialization Unit · 45 minutes**

Read both documents. Answer the questions in Part A, then write the response in Part B.

---

**Document 1 — Andrew Carnegie, "Wealth," *North American Review*, June 1889 (excerpt)**

> The problem of our age is the proper administration of wealth, so that the ties of brotherhood may still bind together the rich and poor in harmonious relationship. The conditions of human life have not only been changed, but revolutionized, within the past few hundred years. … The contrast between the palace of the millionaire and the cottage of the laborer with us to-day measures the change which has come with civilization. This change, however, is not to be deplored, but welcomed as highly beneficial. … Much better this great irregularity than universal squalor.

**Document 2 — Preamble to the Omaha Platform of the People's (Populist) Party, July 1892 (excerpt)**

> We meet in the midst of a nation brought to the verge of moral, political, and material ruin. Corruption dominates the ballot-box, the Legislatures, the Congress, and touches even the ermine of the bench. … The fruits of the toil of millions are boldly stolen to build up colossal fortunes for a few, unprecedented in the history of mankind; and the possessors of these, in turn, despise the Republic and endanger liberty. From the same prolific womb of governmental injustice we breed the two great classes — tramps and millionaires.

---

### Part A — Sourcing and close reading

1. For each document, identify the **author**, the **year**, and the **audience** the author was most likely trying to reach. One line each.

2. Both authors describe the same fact: a large gap between the very rich and everyone else. In one sentence each, state how **Document 1** explains where that gap came from, and how **Document 2** explains where it came from.

   **Before you write:** Which document treats the gap as natural or inevitable? Which treats it as caused by specific human actions or policies? That contrast is the heart of the difference.

3. Choose one word or phrase from each document that shows the author's attitude toward great fortunes. Explain what the word choice reveals.

   **Your explanation should answer:** Does this word make fortunes sound good, bad, dangerous, necessary, or something else? What does that tell you about what the author wants the reader to believe?

4. Why might Carnegie, of all people, have written Document 1? Why might a farmers' and workers' party have written Document 2? Use what you know about each author's position in 1889–1892.

   **Connect position to argument:** Carnegie was __________, so it makes sense he would argue __________. The Populist Party represented __________, so it makes sense they would argue __________.

### Part B — Argument

5. **Question:** In the late nineteenth century, did Americans see the growing gap between rich and poor as the natural price of progress, or as evidence that the system had failed?

   **Before you write your two paragraphs, plan your answer to these questions:**

   - **Prediction:** Based on the two documents, which view do you think was more widely believed in the 1890s, or did different groups believe different things? Write one sentence.

   - **Evidence check:** Which sentence or phrase from Document 1 will you use? Which from Document 2? Write them down.

   - **Weighing sources:** Why should a historian trust or question what Carnegie says? Why should a historian trust or question what the Populist Party says? (Think about what each had to gain.)

   **Now write your two paragraphs** that:
   - take a position that accounts for **both** documents (you may argue that different groups saw it differently — say which and why);
   - use at least one piece of evidence from **each** document;
   - explain how the **point of view** of each author affects how much weight you give their claim;
   - connect the documents to at least one specific development from the period (for example: the growth of trusts, the Homestead or Pullman strikes, farm debt, the 1893 depression).

**Scoring (6 points):** Sourcing accurate (1) · Position addresses both documents (1) · Evidence from each document (1) · Point of view used to weigh evidence (1) · Outside development connected accurately (1) · Organization and clarity (1).
