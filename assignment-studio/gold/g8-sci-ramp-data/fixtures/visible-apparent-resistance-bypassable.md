# Ramp Height and Cart Speed — Data Analysis (Thinking Visible version)

**Grade 8 Physical Science · Energy Unit · 30 minutes · Complete individually, in your own words**

**This version is personalized.** Take the last digit of your student ID number and add it to 40. That is your *personal release height* in cm (between 40 and 49). Use it in Part 3.

A class released a cart from rest at four different heights on the same ramp and measured its speed at the bottom with a photogate. Each height was tested three times.

| Release height (cm) | Trial 1 speed (m/s) | Trial 2 speed (m/s) | Trial 3 speed (m/s) | Average speed (m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1.28 | 1.33 | 1.30 | |
| 20 | 1.84 | 1.87 | 1.82 | |
| 30 | 2.24 | 2.28 | 2.23 | |
| 40 | 2.58 | 2.62 | 2.61 | |

### Part 1 — Organize the data

1. Calculate the average speed for each height and fill in the last column. Round to the nearest hundredth.

2. Sketch a graph of average speed (y-axis) versus release height (x-axis). Label both axes with units.

### Part 2 — Claim, Evidence, Reasoning

3. **Claim.** In your own words, write one sentence stating how the cart's speed at the bottom is related to its release height.

4. **Evidence.** Support your claim with at least two specific numbers from the table. Say what happens to speed when the height doubles from 10 cm to 20 cm, and from 20 cm to 40 cm.

5. **Reasoning.** In your own words, explain *why* the relationship looks the way it does. Use the ideas of gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy in your explanation. Do not copy definitions from the textbook.

### Part 3 — Apply (personalized)

6. A student says, "If we release the cart from 80 cm, it will go twice as fast as from 40 cm." Do you agree? Use the pattern in the data to explain, in your own words.

7. Predict the average speed for **your personal release height** (from the top of the page). Explain how you made your prediction.
