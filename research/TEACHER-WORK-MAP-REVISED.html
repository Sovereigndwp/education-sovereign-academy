1 · C′ vs C-sim vs C-real

Your thesis names a chain: student work → reasoning pattern → teacher insight → appropriate next-day response → teacher decision. The three options differ in how much of that chain they actually demonstrate, and at what data cost.

The key distinction inside "C-sim": synthetic beats de-identified for validation. Two sub-options exist. Synthetic = you author exemplar exit-ticket answers that exhibit known misconceptions (9 show denominator-bias, 2 are careless slips, etc.). De-identified = real answers with names stripped. For proving the engine works, synthetic wins decisively: it carries zero student data and gives you a ground-truth key, so you can measure detection precision/recall — including the false-read case you flagged earlier (a slip mislabeled as a misconception). De-identification is legally fragile (free text re-identifies; small classes; handwriting) and loses ground truth. So C-sim in validation should mean synthetic-first, with de-identified real work kept only as a later realism check on the road to C-real.

Does A + B + C-sim beat A + B + C′ as a first proof?

Yes — for proving the thesis. C′ demonstrates only the back half of your chain (the teacher already did the reasoning; the agent just drafts the response), which is why it reads as commodity planning. C-sim demonstrates the whole chain — student work in, reasoning pattern out, next-day response proposed — and does it with a measurable accuracy number and no PII. That is exactly the "capability demonstrated early without making student PII a prerequisite" you asked for. It answers your concern that A + B alone are too close to MagicSchool/Diffit.

But with one honest reframe you must hold onto: C-sim proves capability, not recovered hours. The teacher never grades synthetic data, so C-sim contributes zero to the measured-time story. That is fine — it means the pilot now carries two different kinds of evidence, and they must be reported separately (see §5): measured time from A and B, and validated reasoning-capability from C-sim. Blending them into one number would be the same sin as the 43-hour claim below.

How C′ and C-sim actually relate — they're not rivals. C-sim validates the detector offline (with a key). C′ is how the downstream half of that same engine touches the live classroom during the pilot without PII (teacher supplies the pattern, agent proposes the response). Together they sequence cleanly into C-real once a data agreement exists. So the recommendation keeps three workflows, with C′ folded in as the live delivery path of the capability C-sim proves — not a fourth thing.

2 · Revised three-workflow recommendation

A · Differentiate-this  +  B · Assessment & key builder  +  C-sim · reasoning-from-work, validated on synthetic responses — with C′ as the live, PII-free delivery of the reteach the engine proposes.
  • A + B carry the measured-time evidence, on the teacher's own materials, zero student data. (Unchanged.)
  • C-sim carries the capability evidence — the full student-work→insight→response chain — with a ground-truth accuracy number and no PII. This is the moat, made visible in week one, and it can be built and shown before a school even signs, because it needs no student data.
  • C′ is not dropped; it is the runtime face of C-sim in the real classroom during the pilot (teacher reports the pattern → engine proposes the next-day response → teacher decides).

This is a strictly better first proof than A + B + C′, because it demonstrates the reasoning capability C′ hides, while keeping the entire PII question out of v1 — and it sets up C-real as the pre-validated v2 upgrade.

3 · Correction — auditing the 43–58 hour claim

Verdict: modeled vendor arithmetic, not an observed workload. It must not be presented as an empirical finding, and I've removed it from the recoverable-hours case.
What it isWhat it is not
A hypothetical calculation from Solved Consulting, a vendor selling an AI-grading product (GRADED+). It models a teacher with 5×28 = 140 students assigning a daily open-response exit ticket, then multiplies responses × an assumed minutes-per-response for "high-quality" feedback.An observed measurement. No teacher was clocked spending 43–58 hours. It is motivated arithmetic from a party that profits if the number is scary.

The decomposition, made explicit: ~140 students × 5 days = ~700 responses/week; at ~4–5 minutes of genuine personalized feedback each ≈ 47–58 hours. Every input is an assumption — that exit tickets are daily and open-response, that "high-quality" feedback is 4–5 min each, that all 140 receive it every day. Change any one and the number collapses.

What is actually supported by evidence: grading ≈ 5–10 hours/week, self-reported, with 95% of teachers taking it home S (Learnosity 2025; EdWeek). Honest use of the 43-hour figure: it is not a workload teachers bear — it is an illustration of why feedback is rationed (teachers cannot give deep feedback to everyone, so they don't). It explains a quality gap, and even then it is modeled. It does not belong in any recoverable-hours total, and I've pulled it from the case.

4 · Correction — the teacher model is provisional only

The 5-section / ~140-student / ~54-hour teacher is a placeholder to shape hypotheses, nothing more. Every hour figure, every score, and the recoverable-time estimate are direction, not magnitude. The pilot teacher's own measured baseline replaces all of them, per workflow. No ranking or guarantee in this document survives contact with a real baseline unchanged, and none should be quoted to a school as fact. Where the earlier map risked reading as findings, treat it as a map of where to look, not what we'll find.

5 · The dual success metric

Success is two questions, and the second gates the first. You cannot win by producing fast output that quietly degrades.

Metric 1 — Verified time reduced (per shipped artifact)

For each artifact the teacher actually uses: baseline minutes (timed: producing it by hand, on a small sample) minus review minutes (timed: read + edit + accept the agent's version). Count only shipped artifacts. Teacher-confirmed, per-artifact, conservative — not a weekly self-estimate, not a survey.

Metric 2 — Quality preserved or improved (observable, not satisfaction)

A per-artifact checklist of observable items, scored by the teacher and spot-audited by an expert. Three of these are hard gates — fail any one and the artifact counts as not preserved, regardless of time saved:

CriterionObservable testGate?
Learning objective preservedDoes each version still target the same stated objective? (yes/no per version)HARD
Cognitive demand preservedIs the differentiated version in the same demand band (e.g. DOK/Bloom), not merely easier to read? (same-band yes/no)HARD
Factual / mathematical correctnessCount of factual or math errors present (must be 0 to ship)HARD
Teacher-required correctionsEdits the teacher had to make before use, categorized: cosmetic / substantive / rejectedheadline quality signal

The single most informative number is the required-correction rate: the share of agent outputs shippable with only cosmetic edits and passing all three hard gates. It measures quality, trust, and (via edit effort) time at once. For C-sim specifically, add detection precision / recall vs. the known key, with the false-positive rate (slip flagged as misconception) reported separately — that is the observable quality metric for the reasoning capability.

The two metrics interact by design: minutes recovered only count when the quality gates pass. "Fast garbage" scores zero. That is the smallest instrument that answers both of your questions and can't be gamed by lowering quality to save time.

6 · What this changes about the 4-week pilot

  • Two evidence streams, reported separately: measured time (A, B, and live C′ reteach) and validated capability (C-sim detection accuracy). Never blended into one figure.
  • C-sim can start before a school signs. Because it's synthetic, the reasoning engine can be validated and demonstrated now, and the accuracy number brought into the pilot conversation. That de-risks the sell and removes the data agreement from the critical path.
  • A synthetic response bank becomes a pilot input — authored blind to the classifier, seeded with distractors and slip-vs-misconception cases and a ground-truth key. This is prep, not student data.
  • The (still-deferred) baseline diary narrows: it times only the in-scope workflows, per artifact, not the whole week — cheaper and more defensible.
  • Success is dual-gated: hours recovered AND required-correction rate under threshold with hard gates passing. One instrument, two numbers, gated.

7 · The strongest argument that this whole three-workflow MVP is still wrong

You're proving teacher value in a watched month. The buyer is an administrator, and neither of the things that actually get a purchase order signed is what this pilot measures.

  • Measured pilots spike, then fade. A 4-week, observed, instrumented pilot invites the Hawthorne effect: teachers try hard while watched, the number looks great, and usage decays in month 3 when attention leaves. "Can we save measured hours in a supervised month" is not the same question as "will teachers still use this unwatched, and will the school renew." The right MVP might be a durable-adoption test (does it become the default way they prep?), not a time-study.
  • The unit of value may be governance, not workflows. The deeper thesis — AI teachers can use safely, with judgment preserved and an audit trail — is bought by a principal, not a teacher. A three-workflow teacher pilot can prove teacher delight and still not prove the thing a principal signs for. In that read, the right MVP is one workflow wrapped in real governance (the Constitution + audit trail + "nothing ships without a teacher"), sold to the administrator, where the product is "your staff can use AI without you lying awake about it."
  • The measurement itself might be the product. If a credible, audited "hours returned and quality preserved" instrument is the thing no competitor offers and superintendents crave, the MVP could be the measurement + governance layer over the AI tools a school already uses (instrument their MagicSchool usage, govern it, report ROI) — not three homegrown workflows you have to build and maintain.

Put sharply: a three-workflow, hours-focused, teacher-facing pilot may win the demo and lose the sale, because it optimizes for teacher time in a supervised month rather than for administrator trust and durable adoption over a term.

My honest read of that objection

It's the strongest one on the board, and I partly concede it: the metric should already carry a durability signal, so add one cheap thing to the pilot — unprompted reuse: in the final week, with instrumentation quiet, does the teacher still choose the agent for a new lesson without being asked? That single observation guards against the spike-then-fade failure without turning the pilot into a six-month study. Where I still hold: you cannot sell governance to a principal with nothing to govern. A + B + C-sim gives the principal a concrete, bounded thing to point the Constitution at, and gives the teacher a reason to keep using it. Governance-first with an empty workflow set is an abstraction; governance wrapped around three real, judgment-preserving workflows is a product. So: keep the three, add the durability signal, and scope the Constitution from what they reveal — but treat "does adoption persist unwatched, and does an administrator trust it" as the questions the next pilot must answer, and say so now rather than discovering it at renewal.

What I need from you (still building nothing)

  • Endorse A + B + C-sim (with C′ as the live delivery path) over A + B + C′ — or tell me the capability proof isn't worth carrying non-time-recovering weight in v1.
  • Confirm the dual metric, especially the three hard quality gates and the required-correction rate as the headline quality number.
  • Say whether the pilot should carry the unprompted-reuse durability signal now, or stay a pure capability+time proof and leave durability to pilot 2.

Only after you settle these would the next artifact — the narrowed baseline + quality-checklist instrument — be worth drafting. Not yet.
