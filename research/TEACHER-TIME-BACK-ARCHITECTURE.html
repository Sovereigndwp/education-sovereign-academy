The reframe, stated plainly

Every teacher is privately reinventing AI: writing prompts, testing tools, worrying about privacy, rebuilding the same materials. That waste is the opportunity. The product is not a better generator. It is the layer that owns the school's governed knowledge, the teacher's workflows, and the measurement of time returned — and lets any model or tool run underneath.

What it is

  • A school-owned agent that turns intent into a work package, not an answer.
  • A governance layer that enforces the school's AI rules (not a PDF nobody reads).
  • A measurement system that reports hours returned to teachers.
  • Model-agnostic — the school can swap the model or bolt in a specialist tool; we own the workflow and the governance.

What it is NOT

  • Not a MagicSchool / Diffit competitor at the generation layer.
  • Not a tool teachers must learn — the agent takes the work, the teacher judges the result.
  • Not an "AI strategy report." The deliverable is a Teacher Time Dashboard a superintendent understands.
  • Not automation of teaching. AI proposes, the teacher decides. Nothing student-facing ships automatically.
The sentence that sells it. "How can we guarantee a school 100 recovered teacher-hours before it buys anything larger?" If that number is credible, it is the wedge. The ultimate product is not AI adoption — it is a measurable reduction in the part of teachers' lives consumed by work that never required a teacher.

The four pillars — and the asset of yours that already powers each

This is why you, specifically, can build this and a generation startup cannot. Every pillar maps to intellectual architecture you already own.

Pillar 1 · Governed Knowledge + the School AI Constitution (enforced)

A short, practical constitution: what AI may do, what needs human review, which data may enter which system, approved sources, parent-communication rules, assessment boundaries, audit trails, and escalation when the AI is uncertain. Crucially, the agents enforce it. A teacher asks "write Sofia's IEP from these notes"; the system recognizes protected student data and the school's rule, and routes her into the approved workflow instead of complying blindly.

⟵ You already built the institutional version: Ariadna. Ariadna governs registries with stable IDs, access tiers and AI-use tiers per artifact, provenance, canonical-source rulings, duplicate/contradiction checks, review dates, and a hard split between what automates on a schedule and what requires owner ratification — plus an evidence bar ("primary artifact inspected," "verification method recorded," "no absence claim without a positive check"). The School AI Constitution is Ariadna, retargeted from your institution to a school's knowledge. The AI-use tier field is already the "may do / review / never touch" classification.

Pillar 2 · The Workflow Library

Not features — jobs. Three to start, each preserving the same learning objective:

  1. Differentiate this. One assignment → standard, simplified-reading, ELL, accommodations, and enrichment versions. The invariant: simplifying the reading must never simplify the mathematics or the thinking.
  2. Build tomorrow. Reads the curriculum sequence, what was taught, and yesterday's evidence → "9 of 23 students confused denominator size with fraction size; here is an 8-minute opener that exposes that before Lesson 4."
  3. Grade → detect → respond. Clusters errors into misconception groups, separates conceptual from arithmetic slips, and drafts the next intervention. Teacher confirms before anything changes.
⟵ build-curriculum already defines the gates that make the "preserve the objective" guarantee real (conceptual-model gate, interaction gate, teacher-key gate). misconception-intelligence already holds the method — distractor-mapped concept inventories, two/three-tier diagnostics, cognitive diagnostic models, refutation text — in the Force Concept Inventory tradition. Workflow 3 is that engine, generalized past Bitcoin/finance to any subject.

Pillar 3 · Preserved Judgment

AI proposes; the teacher decides; nothing reaches a student without a human gate. The constitution routes protected or high-stakes requests into a review path rather than answering them. This is a product guarantee, printed on the box, not a disclaimer.

⟵ owner-approval-delegation. You already designed the AUTO-APPROVE / AUTO-REPAIR / ESCALATE machine with a hard rule that novel or protected decisions escalate to a human, and every judgment traces to governance. Re-point "owner" → "teacher/school" and it is the judgment layer verbatim.

Hard truths — where this breaks, and how each break becomes an asset

You asked me to push. These are the five load-bearing risks. Each is also, handled right, a moat.

1 · The 100-hour guarantee lives or dies on measurement credibility.
"Time saved" is the easiest number in edtech to fake and the hardest to defend. Self-reported minutes are biased; AI can expand work as easily as shrink it (the teacher polishes five versions instead of one); and correlation isn't causation. If the number isn't auditable, a skeptical CFO kills the deal.
→ Asset: measure like Ariadna audits. A real baseline time diary before anything is installed; teacher-verified after-times, not agent-estimated; attribute conservatively (count only workflows the agent touched); publish the method with the number. A credibly modest number beats an impressive unverifiable one. Your evidence-bar culture is the exact thing this needs — most competitors have nothing like it.
2 · "AI OS for teacher work" is a boil-the-ocean claim.
Horizontal platforms die of vagueness. The vision is right; the entry must be ruthlessly narrow.
→ Asset: your own instinct — one department, three workflows, four weeks. Land "Teacher Time Back — Math Dept Edition," prove the hours, then widen. Narrowness is the plan, not a compromise.
3 · The moment you touch IEPs and student work, you are in FERPA / IDEA / state-privacy territory.
Protected data is what makes this valuable and what can sink it. A single leaked IEP ends a district relationship.
→ Asset: the enforced constitution is the buyable feature here. Data-boundary tiers, no-training-on-student-data, data residency, audit trails, and the "route protected requests to the approved workflow" behavior are exactly what a district's counsel needs to say yes. Ariadna's tiers + evidence bar are the head start. Governance isn't overhead; it is the thing you sell.
4 · The cognitive-demand invariant is the hardest guarantee — and the most valuable.
"Differentiate this" is worthless if simplifying the reading quietly simplifies the thinking. That failure is invisible to the teacher until a test reveals it, and generic tools do it constantly.
→ Asset: this is build-curriculum's conceptual-model gate applied to differentiation — a check that the cognitive demand and the learning objective survive every version. Nobody in the generation layer has this. Make it the headline guarantee of Workflow 1.
5 · Model-agnosticism is right, but it means you win on governance, not on the model.
If the value were the model, an OpenAI or Anthropic feature erases you next quarter.
→ Asset: own the workflow, the governed knowledge, and the measurement — the parts that don't commoditize. Let the school bring any model. Your durable IP is the layer, not the generation.

Measurement validity — your demo critique is the core IP

Your critiques of the fractions demo weren't UI notes. They are the exact reasons the established science (Force Concept Inventory, two/three-tier diagnostics) is built the way it is — and getting them right is what makes Workflow 3 trustworthy enough to sell.

What you said

  • One problem per bias → a student can slip and be mislabeled.
  • The number-line feedback used decimals students may not have learned.
  • The "thirds" item wasn't visually clear.
  • The grade heatmap doesn't tell each teacher what to do.

The principle underneath

  • Reliability. A misconception is only flagged when the pattern repeats across ≥2 items and ≥2 representations (area model, number line, set model, symbolic) — the concept-inventory design. One item is a guess; three agreeing items is evidence.
  • Representation-appropriate feedback. Feedback must stay inside what the student has been taught — which means the system must know the pacing. No decimals if decimals aren't taught yet. This is why the diagnostic must sit on the governed knowledge base — your critique and your architecture are the same point.
  • Two-tier probing. Answer + reason (+ confidence) separates a conceptual error from a careless slip — exactly your "false read" worry.
  • Action, not a heatmap. Every cell resolves to a specific next move for that teacher tomorrow, not a color.
The upgrade, concretely: a small per-misconception item bank (3–5 items, mixed representations); flag only on a repeated pattern; a confidence/second-tier "why" on at least one item; feedback filtered by the pacing guide; and a dashboard where each finding is a sentence a teacher can act on ("9 students hold denominator-size = fraction-size; run this 8-minute opener before Lesson 4"). Say "alternative conception" as often as "misconception," and never claim we invented the method — your prior-art file already sets that honesty bar.

I can rebuild the fractions demo to this standard next — multi-item, multi-representation, two-tier, pacing-aware feedback, action-first dashboard — as the concrete proof the deeper thinking works.

Making the 100-hour guarantee safe to make

The guarantee is the wedge only if it can't blow up on you. Four rules make it defensible:

  1. Baseline first. A one-week time diary before anything is installed, per workflow. No baseline, no guarantee.
  2. Count conservatively. Only workflows the agent actually touched; teacher-verified after-times; round down. Publish the method next to the number.
  3. Cap the surface. Guarantee against the three MVP workflows, not "teaching." A bounded promise is a keepable promise.
  4. Make it a floor, not a forecast. "100 hours across the department in the term, or you don't pay for the expansion." The pilot's own dashboard is the referee.
Sold this way, you are not asking a school to believe in AI. You are running a measured, governed, bounded pilot that hands back hours and proves it — and the proof, not the pitch, sells the expansion.
