# 08 — Commercial usefulness review (Part 6)

Evidence for buyer behaviour is inherited from Track 1 and CONTEXT.md; the reading below is **[Inferred]** unless marked.

## Test 1 — "Would I forward this to my Algebra I teachers because there is something in it they could actually use?"

**Yes — with one condition on how it arrives.**

What she would be forwarding, concretely:
- Three one-page checks on linear functions (the largest unit in Algebra I), each usable as-is tomorrow as an exit ticket set, warm-up bank or quiz component. Keys included. Copy-permission on the footer, so nobody has to ask.
- Twenty-four extra forms and the rules for writing more — the thing her teachers complain they never have time to do when the same ticket circulates between periods.
- A one-page map of the unit she can put in front of a department meeting: "what our homework still shows / no longer shows / what covers the gap / what it costs."
- A "how to read the class" table that gives teachers a next move, not just a tally.

Why she would forward rather than file: it costs her nothing, it makes her look useful to her teachers in the first weeks of the year, and it is not asking for anything. The Track-1 finding that teachers name *class minutes* as the first cost is answered on every item, which pre-empts the objection her teachers would raise first. [Observed → applied]

The condition: the email has to say, in one line, *what page to open first*. A director who opens the PDF at p. 4 sees a quiz and stops (07 §8). The cover line should be something like: "Page 2 is the map; pages 4–6 are the tickets; everything is copyable." That is an outreach-copy decision, not a product one, and it belongs in Track 2's 05 file when this is approved.

Where the "yes" is weakest:
- **It is one unit.** A director whose Algebra I team is on Unit 1 (expressions and equations) in September will forward it with "for when you get to linear functions" — a delayed use, weaker signal. Mitigation is a second topic in the sample line, not a change to this one.
- **The contexts are typical, not hers.** If her department uses a specific programme (Illustrative Mathematics, Eureka, Savvas), the map's "what students typically produce" column is approximately right rather than exactly right. She will notice; her teachers will care less.
- **The delegability column may read as an accusation to a teacher whose homework it describes.** The tone rule from the plan ("well designed for a world without these tools") is honoured on p. 1 and p. 2's caption, but the word "Delegable: full" in seven rows is blunt. This is the sentence most likely to make a teacher defensive; it is also the honest one. Keep it; make sure the p. 2 caption stays.

## Test 2 — "After seeing the sample, is it obvious what a larger paid version would give my department that the sample does not?"

**Yes, and it is obvious for the right reason.** The sample is visibly *one topic mapped against typical assignments*. Page 10 shows the scale-up as a table by level, and the last column says what each level lets a department *say*. The gap between sample and paid version is not a crippled sample; it is the two things a cold artifact cannot do:

1. **Map the department's own assignments.** The sample's fourth column is written against generic homework. A paid version reads *their* problem sets, *their* Desmos tasks, *their* unit tests, and writes the gap against what those specifically show — which requires the Track-2 audit reading of what each task was written to measure. Everyone in the room understands why that is different from a generic map.
2. **Coverage across a unit and a course, with the cost totalled per teacher and the uncovered claims listed.** The sample says "14 of 19 claims" as an illustration; a paid version produces the real number and the real list, and re-measures it. That is the department-level number the plan (§10) says a principal can carry to a board — restated here as "share of learning claims resting on independent evidence," per course, with every line's reason next to it.

Also legible, but secondary: a shared library of checks with fresh-form rules across Algebra I / Geometry / Algebra II; prerequisite links so a "not yet" in October points to the claim that failed in September.

**Pricing:** not stated anywhere in the pack. Track 2's 07 file carries an *approved-for-drafting* $6–15k department-sprint anchor, explicitly labelled "not a validation," to be sent only after an unprompted inquiry. Nothing in the Education folder approves pricing for an Evidence Architecture engagement specifically, so the pack does not invent one. The p. 10 language ("what a full Evidence Architecture would add") is written so the sprint offer could be attached to it without contradiction — but attaching it is a Dalia decision and would contaminate the monetization gate if done proactively (Track 2 §07 notes, locked decision 6).

## The two tests together

The value exchange is real (locked decision 6): a teacher gets ~3 hours of item-writing plus a unit map for free, and nothing is withheld to force a purchase — every check in the sample is complete, keyed, and multi-form. The scaling is legible without a pitch. The product is not crippled and does not pretend.

## What would make a director *not* forward it

Recorded as the strongest case against:
- "My teachers already do exit tickets." True (07 §3). The counter is on p. 3 ("what one check can and cannot tell you") and p. 9 (fresh forms), but only if she reads them.
- "This came from a company I've never heard of and it's about AI." The pack never says "AI-resistant," never mentions detection, and the footer says a working secondary teacher wrote and checked it. Whether that lands depends on the sender line and the email — Track 2's outreach copy, not this artifact.
- "It's one unit; send me the course." That is the paid inquiry, and it is the behaviour the plan's monetization gate is looking for (§8: unprompted department inquiries).
- "MagicSchool does this." It does exit tickets; it does not do the map, the negative scope, or verified keys across forms (07 §6–7). A director cannot tell that from a brochure (Track 1) — she can from p. 9, if she compares.

## Revisions made on the strength of this review

- The p. 1 "What is inside" box now names page numbers so a director can route the reader (this was added after the first render, when the student pages sat before the map).
- The footer on every page carries the copy permission, so a forwarded page still carries it.
- The LC-8 row's "we would rather say so than ship an item that pretends" was kept in the director-facing map rather than moved to the teacher notes; it is the line that signals reviewer restraint to a buyer who has seen a hundred generators.

## What this review cannot establish

Whether anyone forwards it. That is the behavioural test the plan (§6–§8) already specifies — submission, reply, unprompted department inquiry — and it has not run. Everything above is a reviewer's read, labelled as such.
