**Where we landed.** We moved off "Teacher Time Back" (teacher-efficiency workflows) because
Brisk shipped Curriculum Intelligence — district curriculum/pacing/rubric grounding — which was
the differentiator we'd claimed. The new direction is **AI-era assessment validity**: audit a
teacher's *assignment* (never student work) and redesign it so student thinking is visible again.

**The one structural reason we chose it:** better AI makes this problem worse, not the product
weaker. Efficiency products get commoditized as models improve; assessment-validity products
become more urgent.

**Evidence already gathered — do not re-research these:**

**Locked decisions — do not reopen without telling me why:**
1. No numerical "AI-proof score." Substitution is a named three-band qualitative judgment with its
   reason attached, labelled a professional judgment, not a measurement.
2. Never accept student work or student PII. Assignments only.
3. The audit belongs to the teacher — never shared with their school without written permission.
4. Wedge = secondary math, with a 20-teacher ELA/history contrast cell.
5. Service first, software later. Sell the outcome, discover the repeated work, then automate.
6. Free deliverable is a real value exchange, not a survey. Behavior is the validation.

**Biggest known risk:** the redesigns *add* teacher work (checkpoints, defences, process evidence)
to teachers already at ~54-hour weeks. A more valid assessment that costs more teacher-minutes will
be admired and not adopted. Test this directly.

## REVISION — 2026-09-04 (read this before the Track prompts)

**v1 of the Stress Test is falsified, not launched.** Any verification artifact that leaves the room (prediction checkpoint, evidence card, outline, draft, log) becomes context for the next AI call. Three of the plan's four mechanisms fail this test. The assignment is no longer the unit to fix.

**New organising question:** if anything sent home can come back through AI, what is the smallest piece of evidence a teacher needs to see for herself before she can reasonably say "yes, this student understands"? Minimum *sufficient evidence of learning* — not authorship, not AI-resistance.

**The artifact is now:** the claim → the delegation test (binary; almost always "yes") → the verification moment (a supervised performance on a novel variant, with pre-stated sufficiency lines and a cost block) → the gradebook question → rationale.

**Decision changes:** #1 kept in spirit, binary in form (bands retired for take-home evidence; qualitative judgment moves to the moment itself). #4 confirmed and strengthened — verification is only cheap where variants are cheap, which is math; this reverses Track 1's advice. #5: research program before service; sprint is ~12 months out.

**New blindspot:** separating learning work from verification is a grading-policy change most teachers don't own. Phase 0 now includes three chair conversations before any teacher outreach.

**All v1 outreach is paused.** Files: `~/Desktop/Education/Track-2-Stress-Test-Kit/v2/` (00 what changed, 01 template, 02 math example with item sheet + keys, 03 Phase 0 revised with thresholds). Track 1 findings still stand; only its wedge recommendation is overruled.
