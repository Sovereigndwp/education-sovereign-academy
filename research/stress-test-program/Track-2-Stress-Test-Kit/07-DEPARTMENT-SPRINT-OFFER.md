## Department Assessment Sprint

**What it is.** Every assessment your department gives — or the 25–50 that matter most — read, audited and redesigned one at a time, by a working secondary teacher, so that each one requires students to show the reasoning it was written to measure. Not a workshop about assessment. Your assessments, changed, with the change explained and the objective preserved.

**What's included**

| | |
|---|---|
| Audit and redesign of 25–50 assessments | Each returned as the same two-page artifact: what it measures · where a current model can now supply that evidence · what thinking must stay visible · one redesigned version with a "what did not change" statement · one verification mechanism with its cost in class and grading minutes · a short rationale. Three-band judgment with reasons, no score. |
| Per-assignment AI-use guidance | For each redesigned task, a one-line student-facing statement of what tool use is and isn't part of the work — written to fit your existing policy, not replace it. |
| Verification formats | The department's set of mechanisms (prediction checkpoints, selected-evidence steps, worked-reasoning artifacts, defense questions), each as a ready-to-copy half-page, with the class-minute and review-minute cost stated. |
| Before/after map | One table: every assessment in scope, its objective, its band before, its band after, its mechanism, its cost. This is the outcome evidence your PD funding now needs to show. |
| Half-day department session | Working session, not a presentation. Teachers bring the redesigned versions and adjust them; we leave with the map agreed. |
| The one number | **Share of assessments requiring observable reasoning** — baseline and after. Defined below. |

**What it costs.** $6,000–$15,000, fixed price, set by scope on the call: roughly $6k for 25 assessments and one session, $15k for 50 with two sessions and a term-later re-measure. That is the range education consultants already bill for comparable work (about $150/hour; 40–80 hours of expert work). It is not a per-student or per-seat price.

**Where the money comes from.** Title II, Part A — professional development that improves teacher effectiveness and is tied to a measurable instructional outcome — or building PD funds. No software, no student data, no data-privacy agreement, no IT procurement. Below most districts' board-approval threshold, so a department chair or C&I director can usually authorize it as a professional-services purchase.

**What the department does not have to do.** Share student work (we never take it). Install anything. Change its grading policy. Adopt a tool.

**What a chair needs to approve**

1. Scope: which 25–50 assessments, from which courses, by when.
2. A one-page statement of work with the fixed price and the deliverables above.
3. Vendor setup: W-9, and whatever your district's small-purchase or professional-services form is.
4. Confirmation from your Title II / federal programs contact that the activity is allowable under your district's Title II plan — we supply the language; they say yes.
5. Who receives what: every teacher owns the audit of their own assessments; the department receives the before/after map and the aggregate number. Individual audits go to the chair only if the teacher agrees in writing.

**The metric, defined so you can audit it.** *Share of assessments requiring observable reasoning* = the number of in-scope assessments where a current model can assist but the evidence of the target reasoning survives because of a named task element (band 3) and a verification mechanism is in place, divided by all in-scope assessments. Baseline is measured on the assessments as they are today; the after figure on the redesigned set. Every band is written with its reason next to it, per assessment, so a department member can check any one of them. It is a professional judgment, not a measurement instrument; we say that on the map, and we invite a teacher from the department to re-judge a sample with us.

**Timeline.** 4–6 weeks from receipt of the assessments to the session. Re-measure, if included, one term later.

**Who does the work.** Dalia [surname] — secondary math teacher, [school/role]. Every audit is read and written by a person; nothing is generated and sent unread.

**To start.** A 30-minute call to scope it. [contact]

## Notes to Dalia (not on the page)

**Every number on this page is an anchor, not a validation.** The plan says so (§10) and the page should stay honest about it if a chair pushes: $150/hr and the $30k/200-hr redesign example are what comparable services cost; nobody has yet paid us $6k. The first sprint should be priced at the bottom of the band and treated as the validation, not the revenue.

**Title II allowability is modeled.** The plan's evidence is that Title II Part A funds PD ($2.19B, 78% of districts) and that districts are tying it to measurable outcomes. Whether *this* activity is allowable under *a given* district's plan is the district's federal-programs director's call, not ours. The page says "we supply the language; they say yes" for that reason. Before the first sprint, get one federal-programs contact to read the page and tell you what they'd need — that's an hour of someone's time and it turns a modeled claim into an observed one. Also: the plan's caveat that Title II elimination has been proposed after 2026-27 applies to every sentence here. Building PD funds are the fallback and should stay on the page.

**Board-approval threshold varies wildly.** "Below most districts' threshold" is a modeled claim. Many districts set the professional-services threshold at $10k–$25k, some at $5k, and some require board approval for any federal-funds contract. A $15k sprint can cross a $10k line. If a chair says "I need board approval," that isn't a no — it's a 4–8 week delay. Ask on the scoping call; log the answer in `monetization_verbatim`.

**The metric has an obvious weakness and the page half-admits it.** We define the bands, we assign them, and then we report an improvement in a number we control. The "invite a teacher to re-judge a sample" line is the mitigation and it should be real: pick five assessments, have a department teacher band them blind, report agreement. If agreement is poor, the metric isn't ready to be carried to a board, and the honest thing is to say so and sell the redesigns on their own merits. Do not put a target number ("from 20% to 80%") on the page — it invites gaming by the mechanism we control.

**The teacher-minutes risk scales with the sprint.** Fifty redesigns, each adding class time, is a department-wide workload change. The before/after map must carry the cost columns (class minutes, review minutes, what was removed) for every assessment, and the half-day session should spend part of its time on *which* redesigns the department will actually run this term. A map where the department picks 20 of 50 to adopt is a success; a map where all 50 are "adopted" and none are run by week six is the §11 failure at scale.

**Stage 4 is where recurrence lives, and it is not on this page.** Multi-department, term-by-term re-measure, teacher-facing policy set — "multiples of the sprint." Don't offer it until one sprint has been delivered and re-measured. The single "re-measure one term later" line in the $15k tier is the seed.

**Do not send this before an inquiry.** Locked decision 6: the free deliverable is a value exchange, and the paid path is evidenced by unprompted inquiries. Sending this page proactively contaminates the monetization gate in §8.
