# Outreach copy — direct email, community post, referral ask

**Status:** draft for Dalia's approval · 2026-09-04. Nothing has been sent. Every piece: short, honest, no hype, no "AI-proof," carries the 48–72 h turnaround and the no-student-work rule. Written in Dalia's voice as a working math teacher, because that is the credibility the plan says the math wedge rests on — if it doesn't sound like her, she should rewrite it, not polish it.

Rules applied to every piece:
- Never "AI-proof," "cheat-proof," "AI-resistant," "detect," "catch."
- Never imply the reader's assessments are bad. The world moved, not their judgment.
- Say what it costs the reader (one file, a few minutes) and what they get (two pages, 48–72 h).
- State the cap (first 100, by hand) — it is true and it is the honest reason to act now.
- One ask per message. The ask is always "send one assignment," never "would you use this?"

## What is deliberately *not* in this copy

- **No urgency theatre.** The cap is real (100, by hand); it's stated once, plainly.
- **No statistics.** "80% of teachers say students use AI on assessments" is true and would work as a hook, and it makes the reader feel accused. Leave it out of outreach; use it with chairs (07).
- **No "protect the integrity of your assessments."** That's Turnitin's frame. Ours is "what it measures now."
- **No mention of the Sovereign Academy's other work.** A math teacher getting an email about assessment doesn't need Bitcoin or financial sovereignty in the signature. Keep the org name; drop everything else.
