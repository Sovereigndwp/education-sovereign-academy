# Track 1 — Summary

2026-09-04 · Full report: `TRACK-1-COMPETITIVE-TEARDOWN.md`

## Honest summary

The whitespace is narrower than the plan assumes, and the moat is not where the plan puts it.

Turnitin Clarity is real, shipping, and already in secondary schools (nearly 100 secondary schools/districts piloted in its first 60 days; a Google Classroom add-on since May 2026; "Multipart Assignments" checkpoints since August 2026). But it is structurally a writing-submission instrument: students must compose inside its editor, it is "well-suited for all types of writing assignments," and it cannot see a math problem set, a lab, or a project. It never touches the assignment itself. That boundary is solid.

What I could not verify as whitespace: assignment-level "AI-resistant" advice already exists as free generative features — MagicSchool's "AI-Resistant Assignment Suggestions" and "Multi-Step Assignment Generator," CK-12's free "AI-Resistant Assignment Generator." Generic, unreviewed, no preservation claim — but a C&I director cannot tell the difference from a brochure. Our free deliverable competes against $0 generators, not against nothing.

Learning First's May 2026 report (source of the 40%/14% figures) now recommends schools "map assessment tasks across subjects/year levels to identify vulnerability levels." That is our Stage 4 product, endorsed as a leader to-do. It also means Solution Tree, McTighe and AI for Education will sell it within a year using relationships we lack.

A $6–15k department engagement sits under the federal micro-purchase threshold ($15,000 since Oct 2025) and under most local board thresholds; professional services are often bid-exempt. It is a signature-and-PO purchase. Window: Sept–Jan planning, June PO spike.

Keep the direction. Redefine the moat: not "assignment-level," which is copyable in weeks, but human-reviewed judgment + preservation claim + run-cost estimate, sold through the PD line.

## Top 5 findings

1. **Turnitin's boundary is writing-only and student-in-editor.** Math, science, projects, and any school without Feedback Studio + LTI 1.3 or Workspace for Education Plus are outside it. "Turnitin needs the student; we need only the task" is our cleanest sentence.
2. **Assignment-level AI-resistance advice is already free** (MagicSchool, CK-12). Brisk Curriculum Intelligence holds the district's standards and pacing, so objective reconstruction is a prompt over data Brisk already owns. A software audit is 1–3 months of work for either; MagicSchool Enterprise already bundles PD.
3. **The buyer is a signature, not a procurement.** $15k federal micro-purchase line; superintendent PO authority to $100k (Union PS); quotes above $25k / bids above $35k (Northbrook D27); professional services frequently bid-exempt. Realistic first paid engagements: Jan–Jun 2027 in-service days from FY26 Title II carryover (100% allowed).
4. **Teachers' language is trust, proof, evidence, defeat — not validity.** "You become a paranoid person." "The war is over — we've lost." "I finally just got to a point where all the work has to be done in the classroom." "Something will have to give… I may cover less content." Best phrase found: NCSM's "When good work stops being evidence." The cost teachers name first is class minutes, not grading minutes.
5. **No PD provider delivers a per-teacher audited artifact.** Solution Tree ("classroom-ready plans"), McTighe (templates, no AI content), AI for Education (prompt library, free webinars), consultant days $2,700–$6,500. Our sprint is priced inside the market band for 2–3 PD days — a different deliverable at the same price, not a premium.
